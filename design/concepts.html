A symptom tracker: names and directions

White, minimal, in the DuoDisco family but with its own character. It opens to today with one big thing to log. You only add the symptom classes that happened that day, so a quiet day is one line and a bad day is as detailed as it needs to be. There's a calendar, and an export for the doctor. All the data on this page is made up.

Names

No “Duo”. Some say pain, some say cycle, some say notes. The top three each have a direction below, but any name can go with any look.

The idea that all three share

1 · Open to todayThe date, the cycle day (worked out from when your period started) and one big button: Log. Below it, anything already logged today.
2 · Pain first, then add only what happenedEvery log starts with pain: how bad (0–10), what kind, where, and when it started. Then you tap to add a symptom class. Classes you don't add don't appear anywhere, not even as blanks.
  • Bleeding & clots
  • Bowel
  • Urinary (UTI-type)
  • Headache
  • Nausea, dizziness, chills
  • Heart (rate, palpitations)
  • Sleep · Energy · Stress
  • Food · Activity
  • Treatment, and whether it helped
  • Notes
3 · Calendar and exportA month view: pain level and bleeding at a glance, with a small mark for each class you logged. Tap a day to see or edit it. Export a spreadsheet for a date range (Excel or CSV) with a summary sheet and only the columns you've used. Everything syncs to Supabase.
Direction A

Twinge

Index cards. Every symptom class has its own riso ink and becomes a little coloured card on the day it's added. The calendar is a grid of tiny day cards with the pain number and a dot for each class, so patterns show up as colour (for example, “the orange dots always come with the 7s”). Space Grotesk and Space Mono: a little clinical, a little zine. Closest to DuoDisco.

Direction B

Ebb

Quiet and calm. One deep claret ink on white, a serif for numbers and dates, and lots of space. Today shows your cycle as a ring with this day marked, and one big button. Logging is a single scrolling page: pain at the top, then “Anything else today?” with the classes as + pills. Each one you tap unfolds into its own section. The export is a tidy printable summary that reads like a letter for a GP or gynaecologist.

Claret, rose, blush, ink
Direction C

Flare

Tap where it hurts. The big thing on Today is a simple body outline (front and back). Tap the spot, drag the heat slider, done: that's a log. Extra classes come up from a sheet of large icons, so adding “Bowel” or “Headache” is one tap. Colour only means heat (yellow to red), so the calendar becomes a heat map you can read across a whole cycle. The most playful of the three, and the quickest on a bad day.
